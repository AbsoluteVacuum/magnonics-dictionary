## Section 1 

## Precession, spin waves, magnons, modes, and states 

### 1.1 Foundational excitations and dynamical counterparts 

**_Spin wave_** is a wave-like collective excitation of a magnetically ordered system. 

**_Magnon_** is the quantised excitation corresponding to a spin wave. 

> See **Editorial choices**

**_Antimagnon_** is a precessional, wave-like, negative-energy excitation of a non-equilibrium magnetic configuration, typically stabilised dynamically. 

**_Helimagnon_** is a magnon or a spin wave associated with a helically ordered magnetic state. 

### 1.2 Propagation character 

**_Propagating spin wave_** is a spin wave that transports energy over a finite distance while maintaining an oscillatory spatial character. 

**_Forward spin wave_** is a spin wave for which the group velocity has a positive projection along the wave vector; in the collinear case, the group velocity and wave vector are parallel. 

**_Backward spin wave_** is a spin wave for which the group velocity has a negative projection along the wave vector; in the collinear case, the group velocity and wave vector are antiparallel. 

**_Nonreciprocal spin wave_** is a spin wave whose propagation characteristics are different for opposite directions of propagation under otherwise equivalent conditions.$^3$ 

> [3] Sometimes, a narrower definition is used: **_Nonreciprocal spin wave_** is a spin wave whose **frequency** is different for opposite propagation directions under otherwise equivalent conditions. 

**_Standing spin wave_** is a spin wave that forms a stationary pattern due to interference of counter-propagating waves. 

**_Evanescent spin wave_** is a spin wave characterised by a spatially decaying amplitude, corresponding to a non-propagating solution of the spin-wave dynamics with a complex wave vector. 

**_Spin-wave tunnelling_** is the passage of spin waves across a region that does not itself support spin-wave propagation at the frequency of the incident mode, typically through evanescent modes or another nonlocal coupling mechanism. 

**_Spin-wave resonant transmission_** is the enhanced transmission of spin waves through a region when the excitation frequency matches that of a resonant state of the region, producing a transmission peak. 



### 1.3 Spin-wave geometries 

**_Damon–Eshbach geometry_** is the configuration in which spin waves in an in plane magnetised medium or waveguide have wave vectors perpendicular (or nearly perpendicular) to the equilibrium magnetisation. 

**_Backward-volume geometry_** is the configuration in which spin waves in an in plane magnetised medium or waveguide have wave vectors collinear (or nearly collinear) with the equilibrium magnetisation. 

**_Forward-volume geometry_** is the configuration in which spin waves propagate in the plane of an out-of-plane magnetised medium or waveguide. 

### 1.4 Dispersion, refraction, and band concepts 

**_Magnonic dispersion_** is the relationship between the frequency or energy of spin waves and their wave vector in a given medium or structure. 

**_Magnonic index_** is an effective refractive index for spin waves, describing how the local properties of a magnetic medium determine spin-wave propagation. 

**_Graded magnonic index_** is a spatially varying effective refractive index for spin waves that arises from continuous nonuniformity of the magnetic medium, such as variations in the internal magnetic field, magnetisation, geometry, or other parameters that determine the local spin-wave dispersion. 

**_Spin-wave time refraction_** is the modification of spin-wave characteristics, most notably frequency, at a temporal interface created by a time-dependent change of the magnonic medium or applied field, while the wave vector is conserved if the system is uniform in space. 

**_Magnonic band structure_** is the set of magnonic bands and band gaps characterising spinwave propagation in a given medium or structure. 

**_Magnonic band_** is a range of frequencies or energies at which spin-wave propagation is allowed in a given medium or structure, perhaps with exception of a finite discrete set of frequencies or energies. 

**_Magnonic pass band_** is a frequency or energy range in which spin-wave transmission or magnon transport through a magnonic medium, structure, or device occurs with relatively low attenuation. 

**_Magnonic stop band_** is a frequency or energy range in which spin-wave transmission or magnon transport through a magnonic medium, structure, or device is suppressed or strongly attenuated. 

**_Magnonic band gap_** is a range of frequencies or energies at which spin-wave propagation is forbidden in a given medium or structure, perhaps with exception of a finite discrete set of frequencies or energies. 

**_Complete magnonic band gap_** is a magnonic band gap in which spin-wave propagation is forbidden for all propagation directions. 

**_Partial (directional) magnonic band gap_** is a magnonic band gap in which spin-wave propagation is forbidden only for specific directions or wave vectors. 

### 1.5 Interaction-dominated classes 

**_Magnetostatic spin wave_ (MSSW)** (or **_dipolar spin wave_** ) is a spin wave whose dispersion is dominated by dipolar interactions.$^4$ 

**_Exchange spin wave_** is a spin wave whose dispersion is dominated by exchange interactions.$^4$ 

**_Dipole-exchange spin wave_** is a spin wave whose dispersion is governed by both dipolar and exchange interactions, neither of which can be neglected.$^4$ 

### 1.6 Spatial character, localisation, confinement, and quantisation 

**_Volume (bulk) spin wave_** is a spin wave that propagates throughout the volume of a medium. 

**_Surface spin wave_** is a spin wave localised near the surface of a magnetic medium. 

**_Damon–Eshbach spin wave_** is a surface spin wave in an in-plane magnetised medium, characterised by dipolar-interaction-induced chirality and surface localisation, which are strongest for propagation with wave vector perpendicular to the equilibrium magnetisation but persist over a range of propagation angles. 

**_Magnetostatic surface spin wave_** is the Damon–Eshbach spin wave in the dipolar-dominated regime.$^4$ 

**_Backward-volume spin wave_** is a volume spin wave with a wave vector collinear (or nearly collinear) with the equilibrium magnetisation in an in-plane magnetised medium or waveguide; in the dipolar regime, its group velocity is opposite (or nearly opposite) to the wave vector. 

**_Backward-volume magnetostatic spin wave_** is the backward-volume spin wave in the dipolar-dominated regime.$^4$ 

**_Forward-volume spin wave_** is a volume spin wave that propagates in the plane of an out-ofplane magnetised medium or waveguide and typically has a group velocity in the same direction as the wave vector. 

**_Forward-volume magnetostatic spin wave_** is the forward-volume spin wave in the dipolar-dominated regime.$^4$ 

**_Spin-wave caustic_** is a narrow, high-intensity spin-wave beam or envelope that forms when anisotropic spin-wave dispersion focuses the energy flow into one or more preferred directions, typically when the group-velocity direction is stationary with respect to the wave vector. 

**_Interface spin wave_** is a spin wave localised at the interface between different magnetic media. 

**_Localised spin wave_** is a spin wave whose amplitude is concentrated in a finite spatial region due to material, geometrical, or field inhomogeneities. 

**_Magnonic defect mode_** is a spin-wave mode localised around a defect or irregularity in a magnonic crystal. 

**_Topological magnonic edge state_** is a spin-wave mode localised at a boundary or interface of a magnonic crystal and protected by the topology of its magnonic band structure. 

**_Confined spin wave_** is a spin wave whose spatial extent is restricted by the geometry or magnetic properties of the structure. 

**_Spin-wave well_** is a region of a magnonic medium in which a local variation of the spin-wave dispersion leads to spin-wave confinement. 

**_Quantised spin-wave mode_** is a spin-wave mode whose allowed wave vectors or frequencies are discretised due to confinement. 

**_Soft mode_** (of a magnetic system) is a magnetic excitation mode whose frequency or energy tends to zero as the system approaches an instability, critical point, or phase transition. 

**_Spin-wave dark mode_** is a spin-wave mode that is effectively isolated from external fields or the environment because its coupling is cancelled or strongly reduced by destructive interference, often due to the mode’s symmetry. 

> [4] Although long-wavelength exchange and dipole-exchange spin waves are often treated within the magnetostatic approximation to Maxwell’s equations, meaning that retardation effects are neglected, the term "magnetostatic spin wave" is conventionally reserved for the dipolar-dominated regime. 

### 1.7 Resonance and pumping phenomena 

**_Kittel mode_** is the spatially uniform (or nearly uniform) precessional mode of a ferromagnetic medium or body, typically associated with ferromagnetic resonance and often regarded as the zero-wave-vector (infinite-wavelength) limit of a spin wave. 

**_Ferromagnetic resonance (FMR)_** is the resonant excitation of the spatially uniform (or nearly uniform) precessional mode of a ferromagnetic medium or body by an oscillatory magnetic field. 

**_Antiferromagnetic resonance_** is the resonant excitation of the spatially uniform (or nearly uniform) precessional modes of an antiferromagnetic medium by an oscillatory magnetic field. 

**_Spin-wave resonance_** is the resonant excitation of a standing spin-wave mode, typically quantised due to confinement or magnetic nonuniformity, by an oscillatory magnetic field. Most often, it refers to **perpendicular standing spin wave (PSSW)** mode in a film, quantised across the thickness of the film.  

**_Schlömann mechanism of spin-wave excitation_** is the resonant generation of spin waves by a uniform or quasi-uniform high-frequency magnetic field in a medium with spatially varying magnonic properties, historically due to a nonuniform static internal magnetic field and, in a broader modern interpretation, due to nonuniformity of the magnonic index. 

**_Two-magnon line broadening_** is an extrinsic contribution to the linewidth of a spin-wave resonance or mode that arises from two-magnon scattering into other spin-wave modes, typically mediated by defects, inhomogeneities, interfaces, or surface roughness. 

**_Parallel pumping_** is a nonlinear spin-wave generation or amplification process in which the pumping oscillatory magnetic field is applied parallel to the equilibrium magnetisation, typically generating or amplifying spin waves at or near half the pump frequency. 

**_Suhl instability_** is a nonlinear spin-wave instability in which a resonantly excited spin-wave mode transfers energy to secondary spin waves above a threshold driving amplitude, typically through first- or second-order magnon interaction processes. 

**_Brillouin light scattering_** (or **_Brillouin-Mandelstam light scattering_** ) is an inelastic lightscattering process in which incident monochromatic light exchanges energy and momentum with low-energy excitations, such as magnons and phonons. 

### 1.8 Magnon transport, magnon-spintronic, and spin-caloritronic phenomena 

**_Magnon current_** is a flux of magnons in the form of either an incoherent magnon population or coherent spin waves. 

**_Magnon spin current_** is a spin current carried by magnons, typically in a magnetically ordered insulator. 

**_Spin pumping_** is the generation of a spin current by the magnetisation dynamics in a magnetic material, typically under ferromagnetic or antiferromagnetic resonance, into an adjacent spintransport medium or spin sink. 

**_Magnon Hall effect_** is the generation of a transverse magnon current, often observed as a transverse thermal current carried by magnons, in response to longitudinal magnon transport. 

**_Magnon spin Hall effect_** is the generation of a transverse magnon spin current in response to longitudinal magnon transport, arising from opposite transverse deflection of magnons with opposite spin angular momentum. 

**_Magnon transfer torque_** is the torque exerted on a magnetic texture or magnetic order parameter by a magnon current through transfer of spin angular momentum carried by magnons. 

**_Magnon diffusion_** is the transport of an incoherent magnon population, driven by spatial gradients of magnon density or temperature. 

**_Magnon chemical potential_** is the thermodynamic chemical potential of a magnon population, characterising its nonequilibrium occupation of magnon states when magnon number is (approximately) conserved. 

**_Spin Seebeck effect_** is the generation of a spin current, often mediated by magnons, in response to a temperature gradient in a magnetic material or across a magnetic interface. 

### 1.9 Nonlinear excitations and coherent collective states 

**_Magnetic soliton_** is a self-localised, particle-like configuration of the magnetisation vector field that retains its form during propagation or temporal evolution. 

**_Spin-wave soliton_** is a localised, self-stabilising spin-wave packet that maintains its shape during propagation due to a balance between dispersion and nonlinearity. 

**_Spin-wave bullet_** is a two-dimensional spin-wave soliton whose frequency lies below the spectrum of linear propagating spin waves, making it evanescent in the rest of the medium. 

**_Dissipative spin-wave soliton_** is a self-localised spin-wave soliton sustained in a driven magnetic system by the simultaneous balance between dispersion and nonlinearity, and between damping and external energy input. 

**_Magnetic droplet soliton_** is a dissipative spin-wave soliton formed by a self-localised region of large-amplitude magnetic precession and sustained via damping compensation by spin torque or another external energy source, typically in a medium with perpendicular magnetic anisotropy. 

**_Magnon Bose-Einstein condensate_** ( **_magnon BEC_** ) is a macroscopically coherent state of magnons occupying a lowest-energy state or set of states, typically under conditions where magnon number is approximately conserved or maintained by pumping. 

**_Spin superfluidity_** is the transport of spin angular momentum by a phase-coherent magnonic state with spin-phase stiffness, often in a manner analogous to superfluid transport. 

**_Magnonic time crystal_** is a macroscopically coherent magnonic state that spontaneously breaks continuous or discrete time-translation symmetry through periodic evolution in time rather than merely follows an external periodic excitation. 

### 1.10 Nonlinear spectral structures and processes 

**_Magnonic frequency comb_** is a frequency-domain structure of regularly spaced spectral lines generated by nonlinear dynamics in a magnonic system. 

**_Three-magnon splitting_** is a nonlinear process in which one magnon decays into two magnons subject to the relevant conservation conditions. 

**_Three-magnon confluence_** is a nonlinear process in which two magnons combine into one magnon subject to the relevant conservation conditions. 

**_Four-magnon scattering_** is a nonlinear process in which two magnons convert into two other magnons subject to the relevant conservation conditions. 

### 1.11 Hybrid excitations 

**_Electromagnon_** is an electric-dipole-active magnetic excitation occurring in materials with magnetoelectric coupling, typically multiferroics. 

**_Magnon-polariton_** is a hybrid excitation resulting from strong coupling and hybridisation between a magnon and an electromagnetic mode. 

**_Cavity magnon-polariton_** is a hybrid excitation resulting from strong coupling and hybridisation between a magnon and an electromagnetic mode of a cavity or resonator. 

**_Magnon-polaron_** is a hybrid excitation resulting from strong coupling and hybridisation between a magnon and a phonon. 

**_Strong coupling in magnonics_** is the regime in which a magnon mode and another mode, such as a photon or phonon, exchange energy more rapidly than they decay, resulting in hybridised modes with an observable normal-mode splitting. 

## Section 2 Magnonics, its subfields and related fields 

### 2.1 Core field definition 

**_Magnonics_** is the science of magnons, spin waves and their applications. 

### 2.2 Subfields by excitation class and physical regime 

**_Classical magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves in the classical regime, where their behaviour is described without reference to quantum effects. 

**_Quantum magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves in the quantum regime and magnons, where their quantised nature and interaction with quantum systems are relevant. 

**_Non-stationary magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in regimes where their properties or dynamics vary in time. 

**_Nonlinear magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in regimes where nonlinear effects are significant. 

**_Non-equilibrium magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons under conditions that deviate from thermodynamic equilibrium, including driven, dissipative, and transient regimes. 

**_Antimagnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies antimagnons and their properties, interactions, and dynamics. 

### 2.3 Subfields by characteristic frequency and timescale 

**_THz magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons with frequencies in the terahertz range. 

**_Ultrafast magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons on ultrafast timescales, often in connection with ultrafast magnetism and optics. 

### 2.4 Subfields by dimensionality and effective dimensionality 

**_1D magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems where their relevant propagation and transport are effectively one-dimensional in space, either physically or by design. 

**_2D magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems where their relevant propagation and transport are effectively two-dimensional in space, either physically or by design. 

**_3D magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems where at least some relevant spin-wave propagation or magnon transport extends in all three spatial dimensions. 

**_4D magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems where their relevant propagation or transport is governed by properties that vary both in space and time. 

**_Synthetic-dimension magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems where their propagation or mode dynamics are described using one or more effective dimensions. 

**_Nanomagnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in nanoscale systems and devices. 

### 2.5 Subfields by material class, magnetic order, and magnetic 

### texture 

**_Ferromagnetic magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves in ferromagnetic systems. 

**_Antiferromagnetic magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in antiferromagnetic and related multisublattice systems. 

**_Altermagnetic magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in altermagnetic materials. 

**_Van der Waals magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in van der Waals magnetic materials and heterostructures, especially in the two-dimensional or few-layer limit. 

**_Domain-wall magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves in systems hosting magnetic domain walls, including spin-wave propagation, scattering, and coupling to domain-wall dynamics. 

**_Skyrmion magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems hosting skyrmionic magnetic textures. 

### 2.6 Subfields by interaction, hybridisation, transport, and topology 

**_Hybrid magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies the interaction of spin waves and magnons with other physical excitations or degrees of freedom. 

**_Cavity magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies the interaction of spin waves and magnons with confined electromagnetic modes. 

**_Optomagnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies the interaction of spin waves and magnons with optical fields, typically in systems with confined or resonant electromagnetic modes. 

**_Photomagnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics concerned with the optical excitation, detection, and manipulation of spin waves and magnons, typically in all-optical or ultrafast measurements. 

**_Electromagnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies electric-dipole-active magnetic excitations, and their properties, interactions, and applications. 

**_Magnon spintronics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in relation to spin currents and spintronic phenomena. 

**_Magnomechanics_** , also referred to as **_magnon-phononics_** , is the subfield of magnonics that studies the interaction of spin waves and magnons with mechanical vibrations or phonons. 

**_Cavity magnomechanics_** is the subfield of magnomechanics that studies coupled systems of spin waves and magnons, confined electromagnetic modes, and mechanical vibrations or phonons. 

**_Magnon spin-caloritronics_** is the subfield of magnonics concerned with the interplay between magnons, spin, and thermal transport phenomena. 

**_Topological magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems whose behaviour is governed by non-trivial topological properties, typically of their magnonic band structure. 

**_Non-Hermitian magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems described by non-Hermitian effective properties. 

### 2.7 Subfields by design, control, and computational paradigm 

**_Graded-index magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in media with spatially varying magnonic properties. 

**_Reconfigurable magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems whose magnonic properties or functions can be reversibly modified without structural changes. 

**_Inverse-design magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies the design of magnonic systems based on desired spin-wave properties or functions. 

**_Neuromorphic magnonics_** is the subfield of magnonics that studies spin waves and magnons in systems designed to emulate or implement neural-network-like functions. 

## Section 3 

## Magnonic devices and applications 

### 3.1 General device classes 

**_Magnonic device_** is a device that derives its primary functionality or purpose from spin waves or magnons. 

**_All-magnonic device_** is a magnonic device in which all operations are performed solely by spin waves and magnons, without conversion to other physical carriers. 

**_Hybrid magnonic device_** is a magnonic device in which spin waves or magnons interact with or are coupled to other physical carriers. 

**_High-frequency communication magnonic device_** is a magnonic device designed for operation, integration, or application in radio-frequency, microwave, millimetre-wave, or terahertz communication systems. 

**_6G magnonic device_** is a high-frequency communication magnonic device intended for sixthgeneration communication systems. 

### 3.2 Control and adaptivity 

**_Reconfigurable magnonic device_** is a magnonic device whose properties or functions can be reversibly modified. 

**_Switchable magnonic device_** is a magnonic device whose properties or functions can be reversibly changed between two or more distinct states. 

**_(Re)programmable magnonic device_** is a magnonic device whose properties or functions can be set, erased, and reset. 

**_Tuneable magnonic device_** is a magnonic device whose magnonic properties or functions can be continuously or quasi-continuously adjusted by external control parameters. 

### 3.3 Functional building blocks and structures 

#### 3.3.1 Signal generation and detection 

**_Magnonic emitter_** is a device that generates spin waves or magnons. 

**_Magnonic transducer_** is a device that converts signals from magnons or spin waves directly into another physical carrier, and/or vice versa. 

**_Magnonic grating coupler_** is a spatially extended magnonic transducer in which coupling between spin waves and another physical signal carrier is coherently enhanced by the transducer’s internal structure, typically exhibiting spatial periodicity. 

**_Resonant magnonic grating coupler_** is a magnonic grating coupler whose coupling enhancement relies on matching the signal frequency to a local resonant mode or modes of its structured magnetic region. 

**_Microwave-to-magnon transducer_** (or **_microwave-to-spin-wave transducer_** ) is a device that converts a microwave or radio-frequency electromagnetic signal directly into a spin-wave or magnon signal. 

**_Magnon-to-microwave transducer_** (or **_spin-wave-to-microwave transducer_** ) is a device that converts a spin-wave or magnon signal directly into a microwave or radio-frequency electromagnetic signal. 

**_Magnonic detector_** is a device that converts spin-wave or magnon signals into measurable electrical, optical, or other physical signals. 

#### 3.3.2 Signal transport and routing 

**_Magnonic conduit_** is a magnonic structure that enables spin-wave or magnon transport between different regions or components of a magnonic system. 

**_Magnonic waveguide_** is a magnonic conduit that directs spin waves along a defined path. 

**_Line-defect magnonic conduit_** is a magnonic conduit formed by a line defect in a magnonic medium, so that spin waves localised at or confined by the defect can propagate along its length, even at frequencies for which their propagation in the surrounding medium is forbidden. 

**_Line-defect magnonic crystal waveguide_** is a line-defect magnonic conduit formed in a magnonic crystal. 

**_Magnonic crystal waveguide_** is a magnonic waveguide whose properties vary periodically along or across the guiding path, so that the dispersion of its guided spin waves reflects this periodicity and may exhibit magnonic band gaps. 

**_Spin-wave bus_** is a medium or interconnection structure that enables transfer of information by spin-wave signals, with information typically encoded in their phase, amplitude, frequency, or a combination of these. 

**_Magnonic interferometer_** is a magnonic device in which one or more spin-wave (magnon) signals are split, guided along distinct paths, and recombined so that their relative phase determines the output through interference. 

**_Magnonic circulator_** is a magnonic device that routes spin-wave signals between multiple ports in a nonreciprocal cyclic manner, such that transmission occurs preferentially from each port to the next in a specified direction. 

**_Magnonic directional coupler_** is a magnonic device that preferentially transfers spin-wave signals from a given input to a selected output spin-wave channel or port. 

**_Magnonic diode_** is a nonreciprocal magnonic device that preferentially transmits spin-wave signals in one direction while suppressing transmission in the opposite direction. 

**_Magnonic valve_** is a magnonic device in which spin-wave transmission is controlled, typically switched or modulated, by external stimuli, often through changes in magnetic configuration. 

**_Magnonic multiplexer_** is a magnonic device that combines multiple input spin-wave signals into a single output spin-wave channel or port. 

**_Magnonic splitter_** is a magnonic device that divides an input spin-wave signal into two or more output spin-wave channels or ports. 

**_Magnonic demultiplexer_** is a magnonic device that separates a spin-wave signal into multiple output spin-wave channels or ports, typically based on a signal property. 

**_Spin-wave fan-out_** is a magnonic signal-routing function or device in which one input spin-wave signal is distributed into multiple output spin-wave channels or paths, typically preserving a defined relation between the outputs’ characteristics. 

**_Magnonic delay line_** is a magnonic device that delays the transmission of a signal, typically by exploiting the finite group velocity of spin waves. 

**_Spin-wave active-ring resonator_** is a resonant system in which a spin-wave delay line is combined with an active feedback loop providing gain, so that spin waves circulate repeatedly and resonant modes are sustained when the round-trip phase delay is an integer multiple of $2 \pi$ . 

**_Spin-wave active-ring oscillator_** is a spin-wave active-ring resonator operated in a regime of self-sustained oscillation, in which gain compensates round-trip losses and sustains a circulating spin-wave mode. 

#### 3.3.3 Signal conditioning and processing 

**_Magnonic resonator_** is a magnonic device that supports resonant spin-wave modes. 

**_Magnonic Fabry–Pérot resonator_** is a magnonic resonator formed by two partially reflecting interfaces that confine spin waves within a finite region of a magnonic medium, where multiple reflections and interference give rise to resonant spin-wave modes. 

**_Chiral magnonic resonator_** is a magnonic resonator in which the resonant spin-wave modes exhibit handedness, manifested through nonreciprocal behaviour. 

**_Magnonic phase shifter_** is a magnonic device that modifies the phase of a spin-wave signal. 

**_Magnonic attenuator_** is a magnonic device that reduces the amplitude of a spin-wave signal. 

**_Magnonic amplifier_** is a magnonic device that increases the amplitude or power of a spin-wave signal. 

**_Magnonic signal repeater_** is a magnonic device that receives a degraded spin-wave signal and regenerates a new, restored spin-wave signal with normalised amplitude and phase for retransmission within a magnonic circuit or network. 

**_All-magnonic repeater_** is a magnonic repeater whose function is performed solely by spin waves and magnons, without conversion to other physical signal carriers within the repeater. 

**_Magnonic limiter_** is a magnonic device that limits the transmitted power of a magnonic signal when the input power exceeds a threshold level. 

**_Magnonic frequency-selective limiter_** is a magnonic limiter that limits the transmitted power of magnonic signals within a defined frequency range. 

**_Magnonic filter_** is a magnonic device that selectively transmits or suppresses spin-wave signals based on their frequency, wavelength, or other properties. 

**_Magnonic frequency converter_** is a magnonic device that changes the frequency of a spinwave signal. 

**_Magnonic modulator_** is a magnonic device that modulates the amplitude, phase, or frequency of a spin-wave signal. 

**_Magnonic neuron_** is a magnonic device that performs neuron-like functions by processing spinwave signals to produce an output based on nonlinear, often threshold-like responses. 

**_Resonant magnonic neuron_** is a magnonic neuron whose neuron-like functions arise from resonant spin-wave modes that enable selective, typically frequency-dependent, nonlinear responses to input signals. 

#### 3.3.4 Information storage 

**_Magnonic memory_** is a magnonic device in which information is stored, addressed, or retrieved using spin-wave or magnonic states. 

### 3.4 Logic and computing devices 

**_Magnonic transistor_** is a magnonic device in which a magnonic signal controls or contributes to controlling the transmission of another magnonic signal. 

**_All-magnonic transistor_** is a magnonic transistor in which all operations are performed solely by spin waves and magnons and without conversion to other physical carriers. 

**_Magnonic logic gate_** is a magnonic device that performs a logical operation on input spin-wave signals to produce an output spin-wave signal. 

**_All-magnonic logic gate_** is a magnonic logic gate in which all operations are performed solely by spin waves and without conversion to other physical carriers. 

**_Magnonic majority gate_** is a magnonic logic gate in which the output logical state is determined by the majority logical state encoded in the input spin-wave signals. 

**_All-magnonic majority gate_** is a magnonic majority gate in which all operations are performed solely by spin waves and without conversion to other physical carriers. 

**_Magnonic arithmetic logic unit_** is a magnonic device that performs arithmetic and logical operations on input spin-wave signals to produce output spin-wave signals. 

**_All-magnonic arithmetic logic unit_** is a magnonic arithmetic logic unit in which all operations are performed solely by spin waves and without conversion to other physical carriers. 

**_Magnonic processor_** is a magnonic device or system that performs computation using spin waves. 

**_Magnonic neural network_** is a device or system that performs neural network-like computation by processing and transmitting spin-wave signals between interconnected magnonic neurons. 

**_Magnonic Ising machine_** is a device that maps optimisation problems onto interacting effective binary degrees of freedom in a magnonic system to find low-energy states of an Ising model. 

### 3.5 System-level concepts 

**_Magnonic circuit_** is an arrangement of interconnected magnonic devices that performs a specific function using spin-wave signals. 

**_Integrated magnonic circuit_** is a magnonic circuit on a common chip or substrate that enables on-chip generation, routing, processing, and detection of spin-wave signals. 

**_All-magnonic integrated circuit_** is an integrated magnonic circuit whose function is performed solely by spin waves and magnons, without conversion to other physical signal carriers within the circuit. 

**_Magnonic computing_** is the use of spin waves to encode, process, and transmit information for computation. 

**_Conventional magnonic computing_** is magnonic computing in which information is processed through sequences of well-defined logical and arithmetic operations. 

**_Unconventional magnonic computing_** is magnonic computing that departs from conventional computing architectures or principles, typically by exploiting alternative paradigms such as wave-based, quantum, neuromorphic, reservoir, or non-Boolean computation. 

**_Magnonic reservoir_** is a nonlinear magnonic system whose intrinsically high-dimensional dynamical response is used as the reservoir in physical reservoir computing. 

**_Magnonic reservoir computing_** is a form of physical reservoir computing in which input signals are processed by mapping them onto the intrinsically high-dimensional nonlinear dynamics of a magnonic reservoir and training the readout from its outputs. 

### 3.6 Sensing and application-oriented devices 

**_Magnonic sensor_** is a magnonic device in which spin waves are used to detect or measure physical, chemical, or biological quantities. 

## Section 4 

## Magnonic crystals, metamaterials, structures, and media 

### 4.1 Magnonic crystals and their dimensional subclasses 

**_Magnonic crystal_** is a medium or structure that supports spin waves and whose magnonic characteristics arise from spatially periodic variation of its properties with a period greater than the atomic unit cell.$^5$ 

> [5] This is proposed to replace the 2025 version of the definition: `` **_Magnonic crystal_** is a periodic medium or structure that supports spin waves and their manipulation enabled by spatially varying properties with a period greater than the atomic unit cell.''

**_1D magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose properties vary periodically along one spatial dimension. 

**_2D magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose properties vary periodically in two spatial dimensions. 

**_3D magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose properties vary periodically in all three spatial dimensions. 

**_1.5D magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose properties vary periodically along one spatial dimension but are also influenced by structure or variation in an additional dimension. 

**_2.5D magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose properties vary periodically in two spatial dimensions but are also influenced by structure or variation in the third dimension. 

### 4.2 Temporal and spatio-temporal media and crystals 

**_Time-modulated magnonic medium_** is a medium or structure that supports spin waves and whose properties vary in time. 

**_Temporal magnonic crystal_** is a medium or structure that supports spin waves and whose magnonic characteristics emerge from a time-periodic variation of its properties, with no requirement of spatial periodicity. 

**_Dynamic magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose magnonic properties (typically those arising from the spatial periodicity) additionally vary in time, but not necessarily periodically. 

**_Spatio-temporal magnonic crystal_** ( **_4D magnonic crystal_** ) is a magnonic crystal whose properties vary periodically in both space and time. 

 

### 4.3 Quasiperiodic, aperiodic, and disordered relatives 

**_Magnonic quasicrystal_** is a medium or structure that supports spin waves and whose magnonic characteristics arise from spatially quasiperiodic variation of its properties, lacking translational periodicity. 

**_Aperiodic magnonic medium_** is a medium or structure that supports spin waves and whose magnonic characteristics arise from non-periodic spatial variation of its properties. 

**_Disordered magnonic medium_** is a medium or structure that supports spin waves and whose magnonic characteristics arise from spatially disordered variation of its properties. 

### 4.4 Metamaterial and effective-medium concepts 

**_Magnonic metamaterial_** is a medium or structure that supports spin waves and whose magnonic properties arise from its superstructure, typically at subwavelength scales, rather than solely from the intrinsic properties of its constituent materials. 

**_Effective magnonic medium_** is a medium whose macroscopic magnonic properties describe the averaged behaviour arising from its subwavelength structure. 

**_Magnetic metasurface_** is a two-dimensional or quasi-two-dimensional structured medium whose electromagnetic or magnonic response arises from its two-dimensional superstructure rather than solely from the intrinsic properties of its constituent materials. 

**_Ferromagnetic metasurface_** is a magnetic metasurface in which the structured magnetic elements are ferromagnetic. 

### 4.5 Hybrid, texture-based, topological, and functional crystals and structures 

**_Hybrid magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose properties arise from the coupling of spin waves or magnons with other physical excitations or degrees of freedom. 

**_Magnon-phononic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose properties arise from the coupling and possible hybridisation of spin waves or magnons with phonons. 

**_Artificial spin ice_** is an array of interacting single-domain nanomagnets arranged in a geometry designed to emulate frustrated spin systems and their emergent collective behaviour. 

**_Spin-ice magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal containing an artificial spin ice whose arrangement and magnetic configuration give the crystal its magnonic properties. 

**_Topological magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose magnonic properties arise from the non-trivial topology of its magnonic band structure. 

**_Reconfigurable spin-ice magnonic crystal_** is a spin-ice magnonic crystal whose magnonic properties can be reversibly modified via changes in the magnetic configuration of the artificial spin ice. 

**_Reconfigurable magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose magnonic properties can be reversibly modified. 

**_Tuneable magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose magnonic properties can be continuously or quasi-continuously adjusted by external control parameters. 

**_(Re)programmable magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose magnonic properties can be set, erased, and reset. 

**_Switchable magnonic crystal_** is a magnonic crystal whose magnonic properties can be reversibly changed between two or more distinct states. 

**_Magnonic heterostructure_** is a structure composed of distinct magnetic or magnonic regions whose interfaces influence spin-wave propagation.

## Relevant generic and auxiliary terms 

### 5.1 Wave-kinematic basics 

**_Wavelength_** is the spatial period of a wave, i.e. the distance over which its phase changes by $2\pi$ . 

**_Wave vector_** is a vector quantity that specifies a wave’s spatial periodicity and direction of phase variation; its magnitude gives the wave number, equal to $2\pi$ divided by the wavelength. 

**_Phase velocity_** is the velocity at which a surface of constant phase propagates; its magnitude is given by the ratio of the wave’s angular frequency to the magnitude of the wave vector; its direction coincides with that of the wave vector. 

**_Group velocity_** is the velocity at which the envelope of a wave packet, and typically the associated energy or information, propagates; it is given by the gradient of the angular frequency in reciprocal space. 

**_Isofrequency curve (or isofrequency contour)_** is a line of constant frequency in reciprocal space. 

**_Isofrequency surface_** is a surface of constant frequency in reciprocal space. 

### 5.2 Mode and spectral basics 

**_Magnonic mode_** is an allowed spatio-temporal spin-wave pattern supported by a medium or structure, usually at a characteristic frequency. 

**_Electromagnetic mode_** is an allowed spatio-temporal pattern of electric and magnetic fields supported by a medium or structure, usually at a characteristic frequency. 

**_Mode profile_** is the spatial distribution of the amplitude and phase of a wave or oscillatory mode in a given medium or structure. 

**_Mode number_** is an integer or set of integers that labels a mode according to its spatial structure, symmetry, or quantisation condition in a given geometry or confinement potential. 

**_Lifetime_** is the characteristic time over which the amplitude, energy, or occupation of a wave, mode, or excitation decays according to a specified convention.$^6$ 

**_Propagation length_** is the characteristic distance over which a propagating wave, mode, or other excitation maintains appreciable amplitude or power before decay. 

**_Attenuation length_** is the characteristic distance over which the amplitude, energy, or occupation of a wave, mode, or excitation decreases according to a specified convention.$^6$ 

**_Coherence length_** is the characteristic distance over which a wave, mode, or other excitation maintains a well-defined phase relation. 

**_Linewidth_** is the spectral width of a resonance or mode, typically measured at a specified fraction of its maximum amplitude or power, and characterising its temporal decay or damping.$^6$ 

**_Full width at half maximum_** ( **_FWHM_** ) of a spectral line, resonance, or mode is the width measured between the two points where the chosen spectral response equals one half of its maximum value. 

**_Radiative linewidth_** is the contribution to the linewidth of a resonance or mode that arises from radiative coupling to the environment or measurement circuit, leading to energy loss by emission or re-radiation rather than by intrinsic damping.$^6$ 

> [6] Lifetime and attenuation length for amplitude and energy decay differ by a factor of 2. Linewidth and quality factor are also convention-dependent, especially with respect to angular versus cyclic frequency and amplitude versus power linewidths. 

**_Inhomogeneous (line or linewidth) broadening_** is an extrinsic contribution to the linewidth of a resonance or mode that arises from spatial or ensemble variations in its local resonance conditions, such as variations in field, anisotropy, composition, thickness, strain, or other structural nonuniformities. 

**_Quality factor_** is a dimensionless measure of the sharpness of a resonance, equal to the ratio of the resonance frequency to its linewidth and related to the number of oscillations occurring within the decay time.$^6$ 

**_Extinction ratio_** is the ratio of the power in the high-signal (or "on") state to that in the lowsignal (or "off") state of a device or modulation scheme, typically expressed either as a linear ratio or in decibels. 

**_Magnetic damping_** is a process in which magnetisation dynamics lose energy and decay as the magnetic system relaxes towards equilibrium. 

**_Gilbert damping_** is a torque term in the Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert equation that describes intrinsic relaxation of the magnetisation towards equilibrium and is proportional to the partial time derivative of the magnetisation, being orthogonal to both the magnetisation and that derivative. 

**_Gilbert damping parameter_** is the dimensionless phenomenological parameter that sets the strength of Gilbert damping in the Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert equation. 

**_Effective Gilbert damping parameter_** is an experimentally obtained Gilbert damping parameter that combines intrinsic Gilbert damping with extrinsic contributions to relaxation, without distinguishing between them. 

**_Spin pinning_** is a phenomenon of reduced spin precession amplitude near the boundary of a magnetic material, associated with boundary conditions which are different from the Neumann-type boundary condition. 

### 5.3 Reciprocal space and topology basics 

**_Reciprocal space_** is the vector space of wave vectors, in which dispersion relations, diffraction conditions, and band structures of translationally symmetric media are naturally described. 

**_Reciprocal lattice_** is the lattice in reciprocal space whose vectors are defined so that their scalar products with the primitive vectors of the direct lattice are integer multiples of $2 \pi$ . 

**_The first Brillouin zone_** (conventionally, just " **_Brillouin zone_** ") is the Wigner–Seitz primitive cell of the reciprocal lattice, consisting of points in reciprocal space that are closer to a chosen reciprocal-lattice point, usually the origin, than to any other. 

**_Bragg diffraction_** is the coherent scattering of waves by a periodic medium or a set of crystal planes such that waves scattered from successive planes interfere constructively and form diffraction maxima for specific relations (known as **_Bragg conditions_** ) between the wave vectors and the arrangement of the planes. 

**_Bragg band gap_** is a gap in the spectrum of a periodic medium that arises when the Bragg conditions are satisfied for back-scattered waves, leading to strong reflection and propagation being forbidden over a certain frequency or energy range. 

### 5.4 Experimental and theoretical techniques 

**_Ferromagnetic resonance (FMR) spectroscopy_** is the use of ferromagnetic resonance to measure the resonance frequency, linewidth, damping, and related characteristics of the uniform or quasi-uniform precessional mode in a magnetic medium or structure. 

**_Smit-Beljers formalism_** is a formalism for ferromagnetic resonance in which the resonance condition is obtained by linearising small-angle precession of the magnetisation about its equilibrium orientation and expressing the resonance frequency in terms of the second derivatives of the magnetic free energy with respect to the magnetisation angles. 

**_Spin-wave resonance spectroscopy_** is the use of spin-wave resonance to measure the spectrum of spin-wave modes, typically quantised, in a magnetic medium or structure. 

**_Vector-network-analyser ferromagnetic resonance (VNA-FMR) spectroscopy_** is a broadband FMR technique that uses a vector network analyser (VNA) to measure the complex microwave response of a magnetic sample, either placed on or integrated with a microwave transmission line, typically as a function of frequency and magnetic field; the technique is also known as **broadband FMR spectroscopy** . 

**_Propagating spin-wave spectroscopy (PSWS)_** is a microwave technique that measures the transmission of propagating spin waves between spatially separated antennas or transducers in order to determine properties such as their spectrum, phase, attenuation, and dispersion relation; the technique is also known as **all-electrical spin-wave spectroscopy (AESWS)** . 

**_Brillouin light scattering spectroscopy_** is an experimental technique that uses Brillouin light scattering to determine the frequencies, wave vectors, scattering cross-sections, and dispersion characteristics of spin waves, acoustic waves, or other solid-state excitations. 

**_Micro-focused Brillouin light scattering microscopy (µ-BLS)_** is a spatially resolved inelastic light-scattering technique that uses Brillouin light scattering with a tightly focused optical spot for local detection and raster imaging of spin waves, acoustic waves, or other solid-state excitations. 

**_Soft-x-ray magnon momentum microscopy_** is a reciprocal-space microscopy technique in which resonant magnetic soft-x-ray scattering is used to detect spin waves or magnons through their diffraction signal, enabling their mapping directly in momentum space. 

**_Time-resolved magneto-optical Kerr effect (TR-MOKE)_** is a pump-probe, time-resolved measurement of the magnetisation dynamics through changes in the polarisation of light reflected from a magnetic material, often with sub-picosecond temporal resolution. 

**_Time-resolved scanning Kerr microscopy (TRSKM)_** is a pump-probe technique that uses the magneto-optical Kerr effect (MOKE) to detect and raster image magnetisation dynamics through the time-dependent Kerr rotation or ellipticity of light reflected from a magnetic sample, typically with picosecond temporal resolution and sub-micrometre spatial resolution. 

**_Time-resolved optically pumped scanning optical microscopy (TROPSOM)_** is a pump– probe technique that uses ultrafast optical pulses to detect and raster image optically excited electron, lattice, and magnetisation dynamics with sub-picosecond temporal and submicrometre spatial resolution. 

**_Super-Nyquist sampling magneto-optical Kerr effect (SNS-MOKE) microscopy_** is a phase-resolved measurement of the coherent magnetisation dynamics through changes in the polarisation of light reflected from a magnetic material, using pulsed laser to capture spin-wave signal samples at a rate lower than the spin-wave frequency. 

**_THz emission spectroscopy_** is a technique in which a sample excited by a femtosecond optical pulse emits terahertz radiation whose time-domain waveform is measured to infer the charge, spin, or structural dynamics that generated it. 

**_THz time-domain spectroscopy (THz-TDS)_** is a technique in which the time-domain waveform of a picosecond electromagnetic pulse transmitted through or reflected from a sample is measured to determine the sample's electromagnetic response at terahertz frequencies. 

**_NV-centre magnetometry_** is a quantum magnetometry technique that uses the spin-dependent optical and microwave response of nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centres in diamond to detect the magnitude and direction of magnetic fields and to image their spatial distribution.
